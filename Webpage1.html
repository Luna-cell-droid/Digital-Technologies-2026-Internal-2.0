Human Causes of Climate Change

Earthbeat: Climate change and actions - a guide to help our planet

There are four major greenhouse gases that are either produced or already on Earth. Now you have a question: what are Greenhouse gases? Greenhouse gases are gases that are present in the atmosphere of the Earth which helps to trap heat of the sun in the atmosphere and keep the planet warm. The four major greenhouse gases are carbon dioxide, methane, Nitrous Oxide, Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs).

Carbon dioxide can be released through two ways. Naturally, it is through volcanic eruptions. However, through human activities such as burning fossil fuels and deforestation it can release carbon dioxide. Methane comes from plant matter breakdown in wetlands and is also released from landfills, rice farming and livestock animals emit methane from their digestion and manure. Nitrous Oxide is produced by farming practices, commercial and organic fertilizer production and use. It also comes from burning fossil fuels and vegetation. Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) are entirely industrial in origin. They are used as refrigerants, solvents and spray can propellants.

 Deforestation and Fossil fuels

In the last century, burning of fossil fuels like coal and oil has increased the concentration of atmospheric carbon dioxide. This happened because the coal or oil burning process combines oxygen and carbon in the air to make carbon dioxide. Other activities include the clearing of land for agriculture, industry and other human activities. Plants absorb Carbon dioxide and because of deforestation, the plants have significantly decreased. This will also cause less carbon dioxide to get absorbed resulting in high greenhouse gas in the atmosphere. The industrial activities that our current civilization depends on have raised atmospheric carbon dioxide levels by nearly 50% in the last century or two. These scientists can see distinctive isotopic fingerprints in the atmosphere to check about the carbon dioxide and other greenhouse gases level.

The 'greenhouse effect' is the warming of climate that results when the atmosphere traps heat radiating from Earth toward space. Gases that are mentioned above resemble glass in a greenhouse, which allows sunlight to pass though but blocks the heat from escaping. This is what happens with the atmosphere of Earth. The heat is not being able to escape into space because of the greenhouse gases and this causes the change in temperature on planet Earth. But what are the consequences of this?
- On average, Earth will become warmer. Some regions may welcome warmer temperatures, but others may not.
- Warmer conditions will probably lead to more evaporation and precipitation overall, but individual regions will vary, some becoming wetter and others dryer.
- A stronger greenhouse effect will probably warm the oceans and partially melt glaciers and other ice, increasing sea level. Ocean water also will expand if it warms, contributing to further sea level rise and this can cause islands to get submerged in water.
- Meanwhile, some crops and other plants may respond favorably to increased atmospheric Carbon Dioxide, growing more vigorously and using water more efficiently. At the same time, higher temperatures and shifting climate patterns may change the areas where crops grow best.
