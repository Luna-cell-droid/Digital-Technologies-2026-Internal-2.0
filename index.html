A climate change is a long term change in the weather patterns and average temperatures that define Earth’s local regional and global climates. Earth’s climate has changed naturally in time but human activities have created a huge part in these changes. Activities such as burning fossil fuels since the 1800s and deforestation became a driving force for modern climate change.

Everyone gets confused with the words weather and climate. Even though they are closely related, they are not the same. Weather is what we see and feel in the short-term, over the span of minutes, hours, or even a couple of days. It is when you look out the window and say “oh it’s sunny!” OR “oh it’s cloudy.” However, Climate describes the conditions of a specific area over a long period of time. Scientists collect data of rainfall, temperature, sunshine, and windspeed to determine the average weather parameters. This can take up to 30 years. These long term observations are called climate normals. Although the weather can change dramatically from day-to-day, long-term climate normals help scientists determine trends and patterns over the span of decades or more.

Climate usually provides a general idea of the conditions we expect during a season or year but it will not tell us what the weather would be like on a single day. Example: climate normals can tell us what is the average rainfall of Brazil but it cannot tell us what the exact amount of rainfall will be in Brazil on 25 of October in 2028. Humans have caused climate change from the late 1800s due to the burning of fossil fuels and deforestation. Nature itself does climate changes to itself but humans have made it far worse, harder to stabilize climate changes. While the sun has played an extraordinary role in the past climate changes, the current evidence shows the climate warming happening right now cannot be explained by the Sun.
